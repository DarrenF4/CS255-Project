# CS255-Project

# Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
  The DriverPass project was created for a client named Liam, who wanted to create a system that filled a void in the market for training students for their driving test. The system would improve the success rate of driver testing by offering online classes, practice test material, and scheduling for in-person driving lessons. The system also needed to include role based access, ability to communicate with the DMV for updated driving requirements/material, secure account management, and admin tools.

# What did you do particularly well?
  Overall, I feel that my choice of wording and the way in which I broke ideas for the system down were strong. I feel that I did well in translating the users needs into clear requirements that are easy to read and follow.

# How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
  I interpreted the user's needs breaking down the information provided by the client through an interview into clear and structured statements and goals. It is important to consider the user's needs when desiging for it determines whether the system will actually solve the problems it was designed for. And if the user is unaccounted for then the system will likely end up being confusing, inefficient, and frustrating to them. Putting an emphasises on the user we can better create features and design workflows that are more aligned and satisfactory.

# How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?
  Having completed the DriverPass project I have learned to approach software design by starting with the user's needs, then taking those needs and translating them into clear requirements. With that in mind it is then important to create clear and through documentation, through means such as the business requirements doc which gives a structured break down of the systems goals, functional, nonfunctional requirements, assumptions, limitations, and more. In the future I would utilize a break down as such along with the myriad of diagrams to keep a systems design organized and easy to follow.
